The easiest start for a new shooter is a 10 m air pistol. It is inexpensive, safe to train with, and teaches the basics: stance, grip, aiming, and trigger control.

## Why start with air

In most cases an air pistol does not require a firearms license, but this depends on your country's law and the power of the model. Check the requirements in advance.

Ammunition is cheap, the sound is quiet, and you can train indoors at 10 m. There is no strong recoil, so you do not learn to flinch. The skills later carry over to a smallbore pistol.

Spend the first months on technique, not on changing weapons. Keeping the same pistol helps you notice your own progress.

## How to choose

There is no perfect model for everyone. Your hand, the event, and your coach decide.

- **Hand.** Hold several pistols for a minute or two each. The weapon should rest in your hand without effort, and your finger should reach the trigger without twisting your wrist.
- **Adjustments.** Adjustable trigger weight and travel are useful, and so are sights adjustable for elevation and windage.
- **Event.** If you plan to compete, check the model against the rules for your event.
- **Service.** Choose a model with spare parts and a gunsmith available.

> [!tip] Coach's advice
> Do not buy a weapon before your first sessions. At a club you can try several pistols, and your coach will tell you what suits you.

## New or used

A used pistol is often smarter than a new one. Check its condition: trigger travel, smooth mechanism action, cylinder seals, and barrel wear. It is better to inspect it together with your coach.

If you buy from a private seller, ask about its history: how it was serviced and whether parts were replaced.

## What to avoid

- Do not buy a weapon more powerful than the event needs.
- Do not chase an expensive model: technique matters more than equipment.
- Do not change the grip or trigger until you master the basic technique.
- Do not buy on online reviews alone.
