Accessories do not replace the weapon or clothing, but they make your position steadier and training and matches more convenient. Some are for everyone, and some only for particular positions and disciplines.

## For the rifle

A *sling* supports the rifle and takes load off your arm in standing, kneeling, and prone. Its length and attachment point depend on the position and on you.

A *mat* gives a level, non-slip surface for prone. For kneeling and standing, shooters use stands and pads so the foot and knee do not slip.

A *rifle stand* lets you leave the weapon on the firing line without it falling. Choose a stable one that does not damage the stock.

## For the pistol

A pistol needs fewer accessories. At the range you need a stand or a table with a rest and a case for carrying the weapon.

> [!danger] Safety
> Use any stand or case only with an unloaded weapon, the chamber open, and the muzzle pointed in a safe direction.

## For spotting and records

A spotting scope helps you see shot holes without walking to the target, and electronic targets replace it at many ranges. A notebook or the app records adjustments and results, so you do not have to keep numbers in your head.

In ShotSCOPE, sight adjustments and notes live in **Profile → Weapons**, and series results go to the diary.

## Where to start

Do not buy everything at once. Start with what your training lacks: for a beginner that is usually a sling or a mat and hearing protection. Add the rest when you know which support or convenience you need, and ask your coach what the club uses.

## Tools and small items

- A set of wrenches that fit your diopter and stock
- Spare apertures and light filters
- Ear plugs or ear protection, and safety glasses
- A case for carrying

Choose accessories for your discipline and check the rules, because competition regulations limit some items. For protective gear, see the article on [[en/Weapons and equipment/Clothing|clothing]].
