Balance is how a rifle's mass is distributed between its front and rear. It decides how steady the weapon feels in your hands and where it tends to drift.

## What balance is

Every rifle has a center of gravity. If it sits forward, the barrel pulls down and your arms tire faster. If it sits back, the rifle feels light but is harder to hold on target.

There is no ideal balance for everyone. It depends on the position, build, and habits of the shooter.

## What balance affects

- **Holding.** A stable rifle moves less, especially standing.
- **Fatigue.** Poor balance makes your muscles work harder.
- **Reaction to the trigger.** When the rifle is balanced, it is easier not to disturb your aim at the moment of the shot.

## How to check balance

Lay the rifle by the forend on your palm or a support and find the point where it rests level. Remember this spot and compare it after every change.

Then take your position and simply hold the rifle for a few minutes. Feel where it pulls: forward, back, or sideways.

> [!tip] Change one thing
> Change one parameter and check the result right away. That way you learn what actually helped.

## How to change balance

Balance is usually changed with weights. They attach to the forend or under the barrel, and sometimes in the butt. A weight at the front moves the center of gravity forward, and one at the rear moves it back.

You can also change balance by moving the stock, the sling, and other parts. Keep in mind that total rifle weight is limited by the rules of the discipline. For current requirements, see [issf-sports.org/rules](https://issf-sports.org/rules) and [[en/Rules and regulations/_index|Rules and regulations]].

## Balance and different positions

In prone, the rifle rests on the sling and the mat, so balance matters less. Standing, the whole mass is on your arms and shoulder, and this is where balance decides a lot.

If you shoot from several positions, look for a compromise and write down the settings for each.
