The grip is the only thing your hand holds a sport pistol with. It decides how steadily the weapon sits in your hand from shot to shot and how your finger works on the trigger.

## Why fitting matters

Every palm is different: size, finger length, shape. A factory grip does not fit everyone. If the weapon sits wrong, your hand compensates with tension, and the sights start to wander.

A good grip gives a repeatable hold: the pistol returns to the same position without effort. That matters more than any other adjustment.

Check the hold with dry-fire: take the weapon, close your eyes, raise it, and open your eyes. If the sights land on the target without correction, the grip fits well.

## Anatomical and adjustable grips

Sport pistols most often use grips that follow the shape of the hand. They come in two kinds.

- **Ready-made anatomical grips.** They come in several sizes, for left and right hands.
- **Custom grips.** They are ordered or carved from a cast of your hand.

Many grips are adjustable: you can change the angle, the position of the finger shelf, and the width. Adjust gradually, one parameter at a time.

## What makes up the hold

The grip affects three contacts between your hand and the weapon.

- The base of the thumb and the palm press against the grip.
- The middle finger, ring finger, and little finger hold the weapon.
- The index finger stays free and works only on the [[en/Weapons and equipment/Trigger|trigger]].

The grip must let your finger rest on the trigger without twisting your wrist. If you have to bend your hand to do it, the grip does not fit.

## What the rules restrict

ISSF rules restrict grip design: for example, it must not support the wrist. Look up exact requirements for shape and size in the current rules: [ISSF](https://issf-sports.org/rules) and [[en/Rules and regulations/_index|rules and regulations]]. Have your coach or a judge check the grip before a match.

> [!note] Fitting
> If a wooden grip is fitted by removing material, there is no going back. Try the adjustments and ready-made sizes first, and leave the cutting to a gunsmith or coach.
