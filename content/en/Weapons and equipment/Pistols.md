A sport pistol does one job: it sends the bullet to the same point again and again while you shoot with one hand. Pistols are built for a specific event, so pistols for different disciplines differ noticeably.

## Main types

ISSF rules describe several groups of pistols. Look up exact event names and equipment requirements in the current rules.

- Air pistol, 4.5 mm caliber, 10 m distance.
- Smallbore pistol chambered for .22 LR rimfire: free pistol at 50 m, rapid fire pistol and sport pistol at 25 m.
- Center fire pistol at 25 m.

Air pistol is the most accessible entry to the sport: cheap ammunition, a short distance, and you can train indoors. Smallbore pistols require a firearms license and a range with the right distance.

## What a pistol consists of

The design is similar across types, and the details differ.

- The **barrel** sets accuracy. Its length and weight affect balance and sight radius.
- The **grip** is your main contact with the weapon. See [[en/Weapons and equipment/Grips|grips]].
- The **trigger mechanism** decides how the shot breaks. See [[en/Weapons and equipment/Trigger|trigger]].
- The **sights**: rear sight and front sight, usually with elevation and windage adjustment.

## Air and smallbore compared

An air pistol runs on compressed air or gas from a cylinder. It has no recoil in the usual sense, but the mechanism gives a slight push, and you learn to control that too. The shot is quiet and the pellets are light.

A smallbore pistol has recoil, noise, and powder. Rapid fire events need a fast return to the aim, so those models have a different grip shape and balance.

## What to check before you buy

The right pistol depends on the event, your height, your hand size, and your budget. Do not buy on reviews alone: hold several models and ask your coach.

- Does the grip fit your hand without tension?
- Can you adjust trigger weight and travel?
- Are spare parts available, and is there a gunsmith who can service the model in your city?
- Does the event you plan to enter allow this pistol?

> [!warning] Rules
> ISSF and national federation rules limit the size, weight, and design of the weapon. Check the model against the current rules before you buy: [ISSF](https://issf-sports.org/rules) and [[en/Rules and regulations/_index|rules and regulations]].
